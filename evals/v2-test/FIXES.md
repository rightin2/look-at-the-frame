# Ten fixes for Look at the Frame and Heat Then Cool (v2)

Ordered by expected impact. None of these fixes has been tested yet: all ten were inferred from the same five tasks and two judges, so see "Validation before adoption" at the end. Each fix keeps the skill's core move: for Look at the Frame, find what every failed attempt held constant and change that, and refuse the opposite or a midpoint unless it also changes that assumption; for Heat Then Cool, re-read the raw evidence without the current belief, generate explanations that differ in kind, and keep only what survives the constraints and a separating check.

## 1. One shared gate: fire only on stuck work, and never run both
- **Skill:** both.
- **Problem:** Both skills ran their full procedure on tasks where nothing had failed. Look at the Frame fired on any either/or; Heat Then Cool's "skip it for simple tasks" sat at the end with no test. Both also fired on the same signal (two failed fixes), with nothing saying which one runs.
- **Evidence:** Heat Then Cool scored 9/9 on the waitlist and essay tasks (a pattern to explain, a draft that did not land) but 7/7 on the decision and research tasks, where no skill scored 9/9. Look at the Frame scored 8 against 9 on both of those. Decision judge: "heavy for someone who just wants to see the shape."
- **Change:** Put the trigger first, as a gate both skills share: two or more fixes, drafts or changes tried (the person's and Claude's, this session) and the result did not move. If the failed attempts were variants of one design, Look at the Frame runs (a growing list of special cases also counts); if they differed in kind, Heat Then Cool runs. Never both. The descriptions name the same split. If the gate is not met, the skill does not apply: ignore everything below it and answer as you would without it. "A surprise", "a draft that does not land" and "special pleading" are gone, since none can be pointed to.

## 2. Do the method in thinking; show only the result
- **Skill:** both.
- **Problem:** The skills tell Claude to print its worksheet: imposed/projected labels, Firm/Inference tags, a "Reframing" announcement, headings that copy the steps, dropped candidates.
- **Evidence:** Judges called the imposed/projected labels jargon on all 5 test 2 tasks and on 2 of 3 test 1 tasks. Waitlist judge: "The Projected, Imposed, Firm and Inference labels add jargon and length without adding much." decision-frame headings followed the steps one for one.
- **Change:** All sorting, labelling and candidate dropping stays internal. The reply uses plain headings built around the person's own items. Never print labels, step names, the worksheet, dropped candidates or a limits section.

## 3. A length ceiling, not a target
- **Skill:** both.
- **Problem:** Every step added a section, and nothing capped length.
- **Evidence:** No skill wrote 487 to 591 words; skill answers ran 620 to 818. Every skill answer over about 640 words lost to no skill. Heat Then Cool won only at 620 and 630 words and lost at 677 and 818. Judges named length in nearly every losing verdict.
- **Change:** No longer than a plain answer to the same request would be, and never over 650 words. Cut any line that does not change what the person does next. There is no minimum: an earlier "450 to 650 words" target would have padded a short honest fix.

## 4. Use only the person's facts; no invented specifics
- **Skill:** both.
- **Problem:** Frame asked for a sample "with invented details clearly marked"; Heat Then Cool asked for a "best guess" at the reader and a cause at the "environment" or "people" level, which pulled in outside context.
- **Evidence:** research-anneal brought in US insurance billing; bug-anneal assumed a clinic; essay-anneal assumed when the fee talk happened; essay-frame's sample "needs bracketed invented details" (both judges, 7/7). In test 1 no skill lost most of its points for inventing a name, a gender and a session length.
- **Change:** Facts about the person's situation (people, places, money, timing, setting) come only from them. General technical or domain knowledge is fine. Use placeholders instead of an invented sample. Never state an unknown as if it were true; in Heat Then Cool, keep a candidate that rests on an unknown only if its check would reveal that unknown. Where a fact is missing, ask at most two questions, each saying what its answer changes.

## 5. Lead with the cause or the clue that matters most
- **Skill:** both.
- **Problem:** Answers opened with an assumption audit or a "Working explanation" block.
- **Evidence:** Winning openings named the cause at once: "You've been adding intake capacity when the constraint is probably outflow" (waitlist, no skill); waitlist-anneal opened on the clue that three different fixes gave the same curve ("Reads the clue correctly", 9/9).
- **Change:** Frame opens on the shared assumption, found by pointing to what every failed attempt kept the same, then one line per failed attempt on why it behaved as it did. Heat Then Cool opens on the observation that most changes the explanation, and calls it decisive only if it rules out the rest.

## 6. Remove the fixed closing sections
- **Skill:** both.
- **Problem:** Frame required a "what this framing can't see" section in every answer; Heat Then Cool required a Confidence line and a "What would prove me wrong" section in every answer.
- **Evidence:** All 5 frame answers had a limits section ("makes it longer than needed", bug judge 2). All 5 anneal answers ended with both closing lines; decision judge: "The confidence section and length feel heavy."
- **Change:** Frame: at most one blind spot, placed where it changes the advice. Heat Then Cool: only when more than one explanation survives, one sentence on what would promote the second.

## 7. Uncertainty in words, not tags or numbers
- **Skill:** both.
- **Problem:** Frame demanded inline firm/inference marks; Heat Then Cool suggested "[verify]" tags and let a confidence figure appear.
- **Evidence:** research-anneal: "moderate (about 65%)" and three [verify] tags; judge: "add noise without evidence." waitlist-frame's Firm/Inference tags were listed as jargon.
- **Change:** No percentages. No per-claim tags. In both skills, flag a recalled figure only when the person will copy it into something. Heat Then Cool reports a check as done only if Claude ran it in this session and can quote its output.

## 8. No guesses about anyone's motives
- **Skill:** Heat Then Cool mainly; stated in both.
- **Problem:** The rule to include an "unlikely" candidate and one at the "people" level surfaced speculation in the reply.
- **Evidence:** decision-anneal: "You've already decided and are looking for permission" ("speculative", judge 1). research-anneal guessed at the colleague's "real reason".
- **Change:** Wild candidates stay in thinking and must survive the constraints to be shown; Claude notes which constraint dropped each one and returns to heating if none survive. One candidate must sit at a different level (data, environment, audience, process), never "people". Any candidate resting on a guess about anyone's motives is dropped.

## 9. Rank causes only when there is a pattern to explain; tie actions to checks
- **Skill:** Heat Then Cool (and the non-firing path of both).
- **Problem:** The ranked list with predictions was required on every reply, so it appeared on decisions and readings where there was nothing to diagnose.
- **Evidence:** Where it fit, it won: waitlist-anneal "ranks the causes and gives each a checkable prediction... The added structure earns its length" and "ties actions to the results". Where it did not, it lost: decision-anneal "feels like analysis of the analysis" (7/7).
- **Change:** If the evidence already singles out one cause, state it and the fix and skip the list. Otherwise show two or three survivors, most likely first, each with the observation that would confirm it, and say so if the original belief survives. Checks go cheapest first, with actions in the form "if X, do A; if Y, do B". For a choice, the gate in fix 1 means the skill does not apply.

## 10. Give the next attempt itself and one quick check
- **Skill:** both.
- **Problem:** Neither skill asked for something the person can run themselves; Frame sometimes offered a menu of reframes.
- **Evidence:** Winners each gave one quick tool: a fill-in-the-blank thesis test (essay, no skill), a decision rule set in advance (research, no skill, 9/9), a single weekly-count table (waitlist-anneal), a short script printing local and UTC dates (bug-frame, 9 in test 1). essay-anneal's "why this spine beat the alternatives", one line each, was praised (9/9).
- **Change:** Give the thing itself (the code, the rewritten draft, the revised plan), not a menu or only a diagnosis; in Frame it must differ in kind from the failed attempts, in Heat Then Cool it builds on the leading explanation. Add one quick check whose result confirms it or points to a named alternative. Argue against alternatives only if the person raised them. At most two questions, each saying what its answer changes.

## Validation before adoption
These ten fixes are untested. The banned specifics, the length ceiling and the output shapes were each read off individual answers in the same five tasks, so a rerun on those tasks would mostly measure fit to the judges' reasons.
- Rerun on at least six held-out tasks the fixes were not drawn from.
- At least two must be failure-free (a plain planning request, a plain either/or), where the right result is an answer no different from no skill.
- Include two stuck bugs where the fix is short, to catch padding.
- Score with a judge from a different model family, blind to condition.
- Adopt only if the skills win on the stuck tasks and tie on the failure-free ones.
