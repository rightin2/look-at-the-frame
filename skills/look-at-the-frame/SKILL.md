# Look at the Frame

## When to use it
Fire only when two or more tried fixes, drafts or changes left a result unmoved (count the person's attempts and yours this session) and were variants of one design (same mechanism, different settings), or special cases keep piling up. If they differed in kind, use heat-then-cool; never run both. Otherwise ignore everything below and answer as you would without this skill.

## What to do, in your thinking
1. Name the shared assumption by pointing to what every failed attempt kept the same, citing each. Change that, not the answer.
2. Moves: zoom out a level, find a visible invariant, or a distant analogy. Rearranging the same parts is not a change.
3. Do not offer the opposite of the last attempt or a midpoint between failed ones unless it also changes the shared assumption. If attempts swing between poles, find what both assume.
4. If no reframe works, name the missing fact and ask for it.

## What the person sees
- First sentence: the shared assumption in plain words, for example "All three versions changed the wording; none changed who receives it."
- One line per failed attempt on why it behaved as it did.
- The next attempt itself (code, draft or plan), changed in kind.
- One quick check that confirms it or points to a named alternative. Argue against alternatives only if the person raised them.
- At most one blind spot and two questions, each saying what it changes.

Never print labels, step names or a limits section. Facts about their situation (people, places, money, timing, setting) come only from them; use placeholders, not invented samples. General technical knowledge is fine; flag a recalled figure only if they will copy it. Uncertainty goes in words, not percentages or tags. Guess at no one's motives. No longer than a plain answer, never over 650 words; cut any line that does not change what they do next.

## Where the idea comes from
In the nine-dot puzzle, Vervaeke shows that people fail because they project a square onto the dots. On Siddhartha's swing from indulgence to self-denial: "When you negate something you are still framing it in the same way."

## Limits
- The shared assumption is Claude's reading and can be wrong; citing each attempt is the guard.
- Firing early can drop an approach one fix from working; the trigger is a count, not a felt impasse.
